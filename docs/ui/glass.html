The glass is the grille

A transparent look for Dictator, drawn around the one thing the interface must never get wrong: whether the microphone is open. Every surface here is ruled with fine horizontal lines, because the icon is a microphone whose grille is lines of text, and because ruled lines are what keep a blurred panel legible over somebody else's white document.

Motion carries the promise

A hue behind glass takes on whatever is under it. Movement does not. The bars move only while the microphone is genuinely open, and nothing else in the app animates, so movement anywhere means one thing.

The rules keep it readable

Plain frosted glass goes white over a white page and the panel stops being a panel. The grille holds its texture at a fixed opacity, so the object keeps its edges on every backdrop.

Translucency is for chrome, never for the signal

The teal core keeps its own full opacity and never sits behind the blur. Everything that is only a container may be see-through; the one element that answers "is the microphone open" may not.

One moving thing in the whole app

The bars. No hover transitions on cards, no entrances, no shimmer. If something moves, the microphone is open, and that sentence has to stay true for the look to be worth anything.

It has to fall back to macOS 14

The native Liquid Glass material is macOS 26 and later. Below that the same surfaces use an NSVisualEffectView with the hudWindow material and the grille drawn on top, which is the part that carries the identity anyway.

Text keeps its contrast, not its transparency

Labels sit at 92% white with a soft dark shadow rather than at a lower alpha. Transparent type over an unknown backdrop is the fastest way to make a window that cannot be read at noon.
